00 — What changed

Three things at once

Stage B started from Stage A's checkpoint at step 480 and changed three things together:

reward
From task − α·|budget − n| to task × clip(α·(budget − n) + δ). Stage A punished writing too little as hard as writing too much. Stage B only punishes writing too much.
prompt
From "Think for N tokens." to "Think for a maximum of N tokens."
settings
δ = 0.85, α = 3.5e-4 for all three sources.

349 steps, 18 evaluations, then stopped. Training was stable throughout — well-formed output never dropped below 99.7%.

Most of the drop was the wording, not the training

The first eval uses the same weights as Stage A's final checkpoint — only the prompt differs. Changing "Think for N" to "Think for a maximum of N" cut on-target from 46.5% to 22.9% with zero training. The model reads "maximum" and writes well under it.

Stage A's calibration does not survive the rewording, and Stage B's reward gives the model no reason to rebuild it.

02 — Why

Nothing pushes back until the answer gets too short

Stage B's length term can only subtract for writing too much. It never adds for writing more:

mult = clip(α·(budget − n) + δ, 0, 1) → 1.0 for anything under budget

83% of rows sit at exactly 1.0 in the final window, peaking at 90% mid-run. For those rows the length term contributes no gradient at all.

The only brake is the task score — the model shrinks until answers start being wrong, then eases back. That is why the curve oscillates between 0.40× and 0.59× rather than settling or collapsing.

If the budget number has to mean what it says, ship Stage A step 480. Ask for 2,000 tokens and you get about 2,000, at every rung from 256 to 6,000. That is the model the project set out to build, and Stage B has not improved on it in that respect.

If you care more about answer quality per token, Stage B's final checkpoint is the better model. It scores higher on almost everything and writes a quarter less. Its budget dial is still perfectly ordered (+0.99 on every eval set) — it just reads "N" as "roughly 0.44 N". You could ship it and ask for double.

The catch is that 0.44 is not stable. It moved between 0.40× and 0.59× across the run, and the correlation fell from 0.90 to 0.78 as it went. A dial whose scale drifts is hard to document.

If you want both

Keep Stage A's symmetric reward and change only the prompt wording, then retrain briefly so the calibration transfers to "a maximum of N". That is a short run, not a new stage, and it is the only path here that gets ceiling semantics without giving up the scale.
