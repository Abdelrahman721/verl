00 — What changed

Three things at once

Stage B started from the Stage A checkpoint at step 480 and changed three things together:

reward
From task − α·|budget − n| to task × clip(α·(budget − n) + δ). Stage A punished writing too little as hard as writing too much. Stage B only punishes writing too much.
prompt
From "Think for N tokens." to "Think for a maximum of N tokens."
settings
δ = 0.85, α = 3.5e-4 for all three sources.

260 steps, 13 evaluations. Training is stable throughout — no spikes, no instability.

The first evaluation, step 0, is the same weights as Stage A's final checkpoint. Only the prompt wording differs. Changing "Think for N" to "Think for a maximum of N" cut the output roughly in half on its own — on-target fell 46.5% → 22.9% with zero training.

The model reads "maximum" and writes well under it. That is reasonable English. But Stage A's calibration does not carry over to the new wording, and Stage B's reward gives it no reason to relearn it.

02 — Why

Nothing pushes back until the answer gets too short to be right

Stage B's length term can only take score away for writing too much. It can never give score for writing more:

mult = clip(α·(budget − n) + δ, 0, 1) → 1.0 for anything under budget

Around 80% of training rows sit at exactly 1.0. For those rows the length term contributes nothing — no gradient tells the model to stop shrinking.

What eventually stopped it was the task score. The model shrank until answers started getting wrong, then came back. That is why the curve bottomed at 0.42× and recovered to ~0.55× rather than running to zero — accuracy is the only floor, and it is a soft one.

04 — The point of Stage B

It still has not delivered what it was for

Stage B exists to make a bigger budget buy a better answer. The test is whether accuracy climbs more steeply with budget than it did at the start. It does not — two of three got flatter.

Accuracy gained per 10× more budget. Step 0 is the Stage A checkpoint under the new wording.
eval setstep 0step 120step 240change
MATH-500+0.085+0.096+0.057−0.028
gsm8k+0.044+0.014+0.005−0.039
IFEval+0.005+0.003+0.025+0.019

MATH-500 still has the steepest slope but it is shallower than when Stage B began. IFEval gained a little from a base of nothing. On balance, no gain.

That matches what the data said before the run started: math is the only slice where thinking longer actually helps, and it is a quarter of the mix. There was never much monotonicity available to buy.

06 — What to do

My read

This is less clear-cut than it looked at step 137. You now have two usable checkpoints that are good at different things.

If you need the budget number to mean what it says, ship Stage A step 480. Ask for 2000 tokens and you get about 2000. That is the model you set out to build.

If you care more about quality per token, Stage B's checkpoint is better in free mode and writes a quarter less. Its budget number is still a reliable dial — the correlation is +0.99 — it just reads "N" as "about half of N". You could ship it and double every budget you ask for.

If you want both

Keep Stage A's symmetric reward and change only the prompt wording. Retrain briefly on "a maximum of N" so the calibration transfers to the new phrasing. You get the ceiling wording, the 0.99× scale, and whatever free-mode gains carry over. That is still the cleanest path.
